House Rules: Etiquette for Halal Play

Play has a place in Islamic life when it brings families closer, refreshes the mind and keeps within good manners. These are the ground rules this page follows. Families and teachers may adjust them to their own scholarly guidance.

  1. Do not act out Prophets or Angels. Many families and schools avoid portraying them out of reverence, so no prompt here requires it. Prompts focus on everyday people, worshippers and pilgrims.
  2. Perform acts of worship with composure. Mime Wudu, Salah and Hajj rites accurately and respectfully. Save the silly moments for other prompts, such as the Daily Life set.
  3. Stay silent and modest. No speaking, whispering, humming or sound effects, and no inappropriate gestures. Everything is communicated through posture and mime.
  4. Keep it kind. Laugh with each other, never at a player's mistake, especially with children learning their prayers.

Sample 20-Minute Madrasa Lesson

Acting out a sequence is a good way to check whether students actually remember the steps. Here is one way to use the Pillars set in class:

  1. Warm-up (3 minutes): Review the steps of Wudu aloud with the class.
  2. Team round (10 minutes): Split into two teams. One student mimes a Wudu or Salah prompt in the correct order, and the team names it. If a step is skipped, the other team can point out what is missing for the point.
  3. Discussion (5 minutes): Ask why each step matters and which one was most often forgotten.
  4. Wrap-up (2 minutes): Share one thing each student will practice at home.

The same format works for Hajj: after acting out Ihram, Tawaf and Safa and Marwa in order, students can retell the journey in their own words.

How to Run a Round

  1. Make two teams (for example, Team Safa and Team Marwa) and choose a set.
  2. The actor reads the prompt privately. Use the picker on this page or point to a line on a printed sheet, without showing it to teammates.
  3. Give a category signal from the table above.
  4. Act without sound. Give 60 seconds, or longer for young children. One point per correct guess.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes these charades halal-friendly?

Every prompt is a respectful human action: worship practices, good manners and community life. The page avoids acting out Prophets or Angels, mockery, music and inappropriate gestures.

Can this be used in a madrasa or weekend school?

Yes. Acting out the steps of Wudu, Salah or Hajj turns memorized sequences into a physical activity, and the sample lesson above gives you a starting plan.

Can we play without screens?

Yes. Print the page (Ctrl+P, or Command+P on Mac). Menus and tools are hidden in the print layout so you can hand out the prompt lists.

Is it suitable for Eid gatherings?

Yes. The sets mix easy prompts for children with deeper ones for teens and adults, so everyone can take part after the Eid meal.

Can we combine sets in a tournament?

Yes. Many groups play six rounds, one prompt from each set, and award a point for each answer guessed within the time limit.
