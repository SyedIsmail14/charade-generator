How to Run a Round

Set up
Split into two small groups. Pick which category set you're playing from above and let the first actor read one line quietly.
Signal
Before acting, give a quick category cue — mime Salah for a prayer line, a circling motion for Hajj, an open book for a Quran-related line.
Act
Body language and expression only, no talking or mouthing words. For multi-step acts like Wudu, act the whole sequence rather than a single frozen pose.
Guess
The team calls out guesses until they land on it, or you agree on a short cap (a slow count works fine) before passing the turn on.
Rotate
Move to the next line down the list and pass the actor role to someone new. Keep going until the set is finished, then switch categories.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this halal?

Yes. Every line is action-based and centred on Islamic practice or character, with nothing that involves music, imagery of living beings, or content outside Islamic guidance.

Can this be used at a madrasa?

Yes — the Pillars, Hajj, and Learning sets were put together with madrasa and Islamic class settings in mind, alongside the more general Daily Life and Values sets for home use.

Do I need the internet during the game?

No. Load the page once, then print it or keep it open on a single phone — nothing about actually playing needs a live connection.

Can we mix categories?

Yes — plenty of groups run a full evening by working through one set per round: Pillars first, then Daily Life, then finishing on Values.

Why a straight list instead of a randomiser: knowing exactly what's coming next makes this easier to run without a phone changing hands constantly — read the line, hand it off, act. It also means the page works for printed handouts at madrasa events or Ramadan gatherings where phones aren't the point of the evening.
