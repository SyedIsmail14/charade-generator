Christmas Charades by Category – Full Guide

🎅 Santa & Elves

Santa and elves are the most iconic Christmas characters and form the backbone of any holiday charades session. Acting out Santa sliding down a chimney, getting stuck halfway, and pushing himself through with one giant wiggle is guaranteed to bring the house down.

🎄 Christmas Traditions

Christmas tradition charades capture the universal moments every family shares during the holiday season. Decorating the tree and arguing about where the star goes, hanging stockings with complete seriousness, or creeping downstairs at 4am to check if Santa has been.

🎬 Christmas Movies

Christmas movie charades are the most competitive rounds because film fans want to guess correctly and win bragging rights. Kevin McCallister's famous screaming mirror face from Home Alone, Buddy the Elf eating cotton balls.

❄️ Winter Activities

Winter activity charades are physically the most entertaining category. Watching someone mime slipping on black ice, scraping a frozen car windscreen at 7am, or catching snowflakes on their tongue with increasing desperation creates genuine physical comedy.

🍪 Holiday Foods

Food charades at Christmas are surprisingly difficult and hilarious. Acting out the difference between stirring a Christmas pudding versus rolling mince pie dough, or attempting to carve a Christmas turkey while the family watches hungrily.

🎶 Christmas Carols

Carol charades are a unique challenge because players must act out the scene or concept of a famous carol without humming or mouthing the tune. Jingle Bells becomes a horse galloping through snowy streets.

🏆 Tips for Playing Christmas Charades

Signal the Category First

Before acting, show your team what type of prompt you have — mime a chimney for Santa, a film reel for a movie, or a snowflake for winter.

Use the Setting, Not Just the Action

Place yourself in front of an imaginary fireplace, a Christmas tree, or a kitchen oven before you start acting. The environment tells your team half the answer.

Act the Emotion

A child's excitement on Christmas morning, the stress of last-minute wrapping, or Scrooge's grumpy miserly walk — emotional acting communicates character and situation together.

Use Seasonal Props

Use a gift bag as Santa's sack, a scarf as a reindeer's harness, or a napkin as a snowball. Physical props make every round clearer and funnier.

Play in Teams for Christmas

Christmas charades works best in teams of 4–6. Larger teams mean more shouts of guesses at once, more laughter per round, and more competitive energy.

Use Full Screen After Dinner

Post-Christmas-dinner charades is a tradition in many households. Use Full Screen mode so the actor holds up the phone and sees the prompt privately.
