How Character Charades Works

Standard charades asks you to mime actions like "brushing teeth" or "painting a fence". Character charades asks you to act out a whole identity: a superhero, a movie icon, a fairy tale villain or a video game mascot. The actor stays silent while teammates guess the name before the timer runs out.

This generator draws a random character from the mode you choose. Press Generate to reveal 1, 2 or 3 cards and use the built-in 30-second timer to keep each turn fast. Choose Easy for family games, or pick a themed mode (Superheroes, Movies, Fairy Tale, Video Games or Classic TV) to match the evening.

Category Cues: Tell Your Team Where to Look

Before you act the character, signal the category. This narrows your team's guesses from thousands of names to a small group. Agree on these gestures with your group first.

Comic / Superhero

Cross your wrists at the chest, or flick an imaginary cape behind your shoulders.

Movie

Hold one hand to your eye like a camera and crank the other hand beside it.

Video Game

Hold an imaginary controller and tap the buttons with both thumbs.

Fairy Tale

Place an imaginary crown on your head, or swish a magic wand.

TV Show

Trace a rectangle in the air with your index fingers to draw a screen.

The 3-Step Method for a 30-Second Turn

  1. Show size and posture (first 10 seconds). Crouch if the character is small. Widen your shoulders and walk heavily if they are huge. Posture alone can rule out most wrong guesses.
  2. Add the signature item (next 10 seconds). Mime putting on glasses, tipping a hat, drawing a sword or strapping on a jetpack. Pick the one item people associate with the character.
  3. Perform the famous moment (last 10 seconds). Act out what the character is best known for: swinging between buildings, hopping through a pipe, or running from a clock striking midnight.

If your team is stuck, switch to a different step instead of repeating the same gesture. A new angle often unlocks the answer.

Rules for a Fair Game

  • No sound. No speaking, whispering, humming theme songs or sound effects.
  • No spelling or writing. No tracing letters in the air or on surfaces.
  • No props. Mime imaginary objects. Agree beforehand if clothing you are already wearing can be used, such as turning up a collar.
  • Scoring. One point per correct guess within the time limit. If time runs out, you can let the other team steal with one guess.
  • Longer turns for younger players. Agree on 60 seconds for kids and keep time yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can players hum or whistle theme songs?

No. In standard charades the actor stays silent, which includes humming, whistling and sound effects. All clues must be visual.

Can I use props?

Most groups play without props and let actors mime imaginary ones. Agree on this before the first round so it stays fair.

Which mode is best for kids?

Start with Easy, which sticks to widely known characters, then move to the themed modes once everyone has warmed up.

What if nobody knows the character?

Press Generate to draw a new card. Skipping a character is better than letting the turn stall.
